# C1509: Three-tier atom behavioral stability across A and B

**Tier:** 2
**Scope:** GLOBAL, A->B, MIDDLE, atom, behavioral, stability, correlation, cross-system
**Phase:** BRIDGE_ATOM_STABILITY (Phase 539)
**Dependencies:** C1499, C1503, C1195, C1207, C1409

## Constraint

Individual atoms within bridge MIDDLEs show three tiers of behavioral stability across Currier A and Currier B, measured by category-profile correlation and JSD across the two systems. Mean correlation = 0.789, but the distribution is trimodal:

**Tier 1 -- STABLE (correlation > 0.90, JSD < 0.05):** r (1.000, JSD=0.000), s (1.000, JSD=0.000), t (0.988, JSD=0.011), l (0.979, JSD=0.022), a (0.973, JSD=0.013), m (0.964, JSD=0.040), o (0.938, JSD=0.014), g (1.000, JSD=0.000). These 8 atoms maintain identical category profiles in both systems.

**Tier 2 -- MODERATE (correlation 0.70-0.90, JSD 0.05-0.15):** k (0.871, JSD=0.090), c (0.805, JSD=0.052), p (0.799, JSD=0.053), i (0.752, JSD=0.091), e (0.720, JSD=0.147), n (0.717, JSD=0.098). These 6 atoms shift category emphasis but maintain the same overall profile shape.

**Tier 3 -- UNSTABLE (correlation < 0.70, JSD > 0.15):** y (0.476, JSD=0.264), h (0.365, JSD=0.188), d (0.062, JSD=0.405). These 3 atoms change dominant category between systems.

d is the extreme outlier: correlation 0.062, JSD 0.405. In A, d's top category is CONTAINMENT (44.4%); in B, d's top category is OPERATION (55.8%). This is driven by the edy MIDDLE (d as modifier of e-HEAD) being 277x B-enriched, pulling d-atom tokens overwhelmingly into B's OPERATION category. The d-atom effectively changes its functional role across systems.

10/17 atoms have the same top category in both systems. The 7 mismatches concentrate in Tier 2-3 atoms.

This extends C1409 (suffix atoms diverge from MIDDLE-terminal atoms) to the cross-system dimension: atoms are not uniformly stable across A and B. The structural atoms (r, s, t, l, a, m, o) that define frame endpoints and flow are system-invariant. The operational atoms (k, e, h) and boundary atoms (d, y) are system-sensitive, reflecting their role in differentiating the systems' distinct functional emphases.
