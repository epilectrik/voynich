# C1274: AZC Category Composition Predicts B Escape Rate

**Tier:** 2
**Scope:** AZC, A->B
**Phase:** AZC_CATEGORY_SCATTERSHOT (Phase 453)
**Date:** 2026-02-24

## Statement

Operational category composition of AZC-shared vocabulary per B folio predicts escape dynamics (qo-prefix rate). THERMAL correlates strongly with high escape (rho=+0.780, p<0.001). TRANSITION anti-correlates (rho=-0.598, p<0.001). CONTAINMENT (rho=-0.399, p<0.001) and MARKING (rho=-0.349, p=0.001) also anti-correlate. 5/8 categories significant at p<0.05, 4 surviving Bonferroni. Across 82 B folios, the category mix of vocabulary shared with AZC predicts B's escape behavior.

## Architecture

- **Category is the bridge between AZC structure and B dynamics.** C468 established 28x escape rate difference by AZC position. C1274 identifies the mechanism: THERMAL vocabulary enables escape, TRANSITION vocabulary suppresses it. The categories are not just organizational labels -- they predict downstream behavior.
- **THERMAL = escape-permissive.** rho=+0.780 is the strongest single-category predictor. B folios whose AZC-shared vocabulary is THERMAL-heavy have high qo-prefix (escape) rates. This connects the AZC-exclusive vocabulary profile (C1273, THERMAL 27.0%) to escape mechanics.
- **TRANSITION = escape-restrictive.** rho=-0.598. TRANSITION-dominated folios have low escape. Since TRANSITION is the dominant category (25%), this creates the baseline low-escape regime that THERMAL content locally overrides.
- **Connects three systems.** A's category organization (C1261) flows through AZC's zone structure (C1269) to predict B's execution dynamics (C1274). The category system is a cross-system organizing principle.
