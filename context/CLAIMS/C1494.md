# C1494: Displaced HEAD k/t enrichment with inverted frequency

**Tier:** 2
**Scope:** B, MIDDLE, headless, displaced, HEAD, k, t, e, frequency, inversion
**Phase:** DISPLACED_HEAD_GRAMMAR (Phase 537)
**Dependencies:** C1478, C1488, C1493, C1394

## Constraint

HEAD-set atoms {a,e,o,k,t} appearing at non-initial positions in headless compound MIDDLEs show an INVERTED frequency distribution relative to canonical HEAD usage. k is 5.31x enriched (42.0% displaced vs 7.9% canonical), t is 6.90x enriched (18.5% vs 2.7%), while e is 0.26x depleted (12.2% vs 47.8%). Population: 1,182 tokens (35.7% of headless), 334 types. 86.2% of displaced HEAD atoms appear at position 1, with rapid decay (position 2: 13.9%, position 3: 11.2%).

## Key Numbers

| HEAD atom | Displaced % | Canonical % | Enrichment |
|-----------|------------|-------------|------------|
| k | 42.0% | 7.9% | 5.31x |
| t | 18.5% | 2.7% | 6.90x |
| o | 14.6% | 18.1% | 0.81x |
| a | 12.6% | 23.5% | 0.54x |
| e | 12.2% | 47.8% | 0.26x |

Multi-displaced (2+ HEAD atoms in one MIDDLE): 166 tokens (14.0%).

## Evidence

The enrichment pattern maps directly to the k/t dual-role property (C1478, C1394): k and t function as TERMINAL atoms when not in position 0, so they naturally appear in non-initial positions. e, which is almost never terminal (C1209: 0.8% terminal), is strongly depleted because it has no non-HEAD role to fill. a and o show intermediate depletion, consistent with their occasional terminal/modifier usage.

## Falsification

Would be falsified if displaced HEAD frequency matched canonical HEAD frequency (i.e., e dominated displacement as it dominates canonical HEAD usage).
