# C1504: Modifier grammar universality across channels

**Tier:** 2
**Scope:** GLOBAL, MIDDLE, atom, modifier, universality, channel, grammar, cross-system
**Phase:** CROSS_LAYER_ATOM_DECOMPOSITION (Phase 538)
**Dependencies:** C1393, C1394, C1472, C1499

## Constraint

All pipeline channels use the same 6 modifier atoms {p, c, i, f, d, s} with c as the dominant modifier in every channel. Modifier distribution JSD between non-bridge channels averages only 0.0042 -- near-identical modifier grammar across all systems. Modifier participation rates are universally high (bridge 77.7% lowest, all others >96%).

Bridge is again the outlier (MOD JSD to others: 0.074-0.088), driven by its lower modifier rate (77.7%) and different modifier proportions (higher i and lower d/s than other channels), reflecting its shorter mean length (2.27 atoms vs 3.33-4.52 for other channels).

Bridge anomalies: f=0% (unique absence), i=30.2% (highest of any channel, 1.76x B baseline), s=3.8% (lowest).

c dominance is universal: ranges from 26.1% (b_only) to 37.7% (bridge). This confirms the modifier grammar established by C1472 operates as a cross-system invariant.

## Falsification

Would be falsified if any channel used a modifier atom absent from other channels, or if modifier distributions showed JSD > 0.05 between non-bridge channels.
