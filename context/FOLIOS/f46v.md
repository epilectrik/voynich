### Two Candidate Processes

**A. Camphor purification (original hypothesis)**
- Sublimation: heat crude solid, vapor condenses on cooler upper vessel surfaces
- Requires very narrow temperature band (~175C) — too low = no sublimation, too high = decomposition
- Camphor was one of the most expensive single-ingredient pharmaceuticals in 14th-century Europe
- Imported from Borneo/Sumatra; purification via sublimation was a trade secret
- **Problem:** Illustration doesn't match — camphor comes from a smooth-leaved laurel tree

**B. Thistle-family plant salt sublimation (revised hypothesis)**
- Medieval pharmacy extracted purified salts from calcinated plant material
- Process: burn plant to ash, leach with water, evaporate to crude salt, SUBLIME to purify
- The volatile ammonium fraction was considered the "spirit" of the plant — the most medicinally active part
- Blessed thistle (Cnicus benedictus) was a premiere pharmaceutical thistle — near-panacea status
- Its sal benedicti (calcinated salt) required precision sublimation for purification
- Milk thistle (Silybum marianum) was the standard liver herb; artichoke (Cynara) another Asteraceae with precision-temperature preparations
- Sal ammoniac (NH4Cl) was also produced from nitrogen-rich plant ashes (including thistles) by sublimation
- **Advantage:** Illustration MATCHES — spiny toothed plant with round flower heads IS a thistle-family plant
- **Same processing profile as camphor:** narrow temperature window, one-shot sublimation, crystal formation endpoint
