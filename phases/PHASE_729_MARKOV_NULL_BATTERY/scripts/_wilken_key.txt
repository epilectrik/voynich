LAYER 15 — STANDING LIMITATIONS (NEW v4.0)
==========================================================================
======
v4.0 NOTE: These five limitations define the permanent boundaries of the
current framework. They are not resolvable within the Wilken Key alone —
each requires external input (specialist review, embargo release,
independent mathematical derivation, or corpus study). They are documented
here as standing constraints, not failures.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitation 1 — HAPAX CLUSTERS
Hapax clusters (tokens appearing only once or twice in the full corpus)
remain unresolved. The framework does not attempt to assign meanings to
hapax tokens. They are flagged and deferred. Forcing hapax resolutions
risks corrupting higher-confidence cluster assignments.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitation 2 — GRAMMATICAL vs LEXICAL AMBIGUITY
Very common words may be grammatical particles rather than lexical content
words. High-frequency tokens whose meaning appears context-independent
should be treated with caution and classified as probable grammatical
markers until confirmed otherwise. The P-gallows case (v2.0) demonstrates
how a token that appeared to carry phase-marking content was revealed
through blind testing to be a grammatical discourse particle.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitation 3 — SCRIBAL VARIATION
Scribal variation may affect cluster forms across the manuscript's multiple
hands (at least 7 hands confirmed in MS 15; comparable variation expected
in the Voynich). The same phonetic value may be encoded with slightly
differing glyph forms by different scribes. Cluster matching must account
for this variation rather than treating orthographic differences as
===== PAGE 40 =====
semantic differences.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitation 4 — SPECULATIVE MAPPINGS
Some proposed mappings are explicitly speculative, particularly those
dependent on consonant softening as the primary phonetic justification
(auo/uisce is the primary flagged case). These are retained in the lexicon
at MED-HIGH confidence with explicit Celtic Studies review flagging. They
are not cited as evidence for other claims.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Limitation 5 — SYSTEM NOT REDUCIBLE TO NATURAL LANGUAGE
The manuscript may encode a system that is not fully reducible to natural
language. The framework's current architecture assumes a bilingual (Irish /
Old Italian) procedural text. If the Soyga generative mechanism (Component J)
or an equivalent algorithmic process is confirmed, the text may be better
described as a rule-generated encoding of semantic primitives rather than
a natural language text. This possibility does not invalidate the Irish
procedural layer — it would reframe it as a semantic key imposed on a
generated substrate, not a directly spoken or written natural language
encoded in cipher.
