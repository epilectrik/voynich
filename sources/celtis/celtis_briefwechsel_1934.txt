INTRODUCTION (EXCERPTS)
================================================================================

[p. V] The introduction opens with a Goethe/Winckelmann quotation about the
importance of letters as historical monuments. Konrad Celtis (1459-1508) is
introduced, and the history of efforts to publish his correspondence is
discussed. The Codex epistolaris (Cod. 3448) in the Vienna National Library
is described as the primary source, with paleographic details.

[p. XI] Manuscript sources catalogued: 19 manuscript locations including
holdings in Munich, Nuremberg, Karlsruhe, Kremsmuenster, and Lambach.
Early printed editions used as sources include:
- Celtis's Ars versificandi and Proseuticum
- His Seneca edition and rhetorical writings
- His Amores, Rhapsodia, and Ligurinus
- Works by Hrosvita and Apuleius
- Pirckheimer's Epistolae Charitatis
- Trithemius's Epistolae familiares

[p. XII] The editor (Rupprich) began collecting material in 1924; official
commission from the Academy of Sciences in Munich received in 1928. Total
corpus: 266 letters from 125 authors in the Codex epistolaris, plus ~100
additional from other MSS and printed editions = 359 items. Editorial
principles follow established conventions for Latin text presentation.

LETTER 88: Johannes Trithemius to Konrad Celtis
Date: April 11, 1495
Location: Frankfurt am Main
Source: Cod. ep. V, 4, fol. 43rN
Pages: 144-147
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 144-145] The letter confirms receipt of a prior communication from Celtis.
Trithemius requests assistance acquiring Greek works, offering payment through
the letter's bearer. He reports progress in Greek studies under Johann
Reuchlin's instruction. He mentions receiving a Greek psalter from the Bishop
of Worms (Johann von Dalberg).

Key Latin passage:
"tuus sum et ego totus et mea omnia"
(I am entirely yours, and all I have is yours)

Trithemius invites Celtis to visit during the summer, promising financial
support. He defends the Bishop of Worms against rumors of dismissal from
court. He mentions Johannes Amerbach will soon publish poets' works.

[p. 146] Discussion of multiple princes and uncertain affairs. Reference to
Johannes de Drade (Hans vom Drat), a marshal at the Palatinate court, who
was excommunicated by the Abbot of Weissenburg for violent actions against
the monastery. Account of Theodoricus Gresmundus (Dietrich Gresemund the
Younger, 1475-1512), a humanist who initially sought monastic life but
returned home after a domestic scandal. Footnote: Gresemund's works were
dedicated to Trithemius (printed Mainz 1494).

Mentions Jacobus Wimpfeling serving as a preacher in Speyer. References to
Rutgerus the Sycamber and printing works being sent.

[p. 147] Conclusion: Discussion of a catalog of illustrious German figures
that Celtis prepared at Jakob Wimpheling's request, soon to be printed with
additional entries. Johannes Vigilius staying at the same lodging sends
greetings.

Closing: "3. Idus Aprilis anno Domini 95" (April 11, 1495) from Frankfurt.

BIOGRAPHICAL NOTE (from Rupprich's apparatus):
Johannes Trithemius (1462-1516) was a Benedictine abbot at Sponheim
monastery, theologian, and polymath. He maintained close friendship with
Celtis, who taught him Greek and dedicated multiple poetic works to him.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
LETTER 89: Johannes Vigilius to Konrad Celtis
Date: April 7 [sic; 11?], 1495
Pages: 147-148
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[Immediately following Letter 88]
Vigilius invites Celtis to prepare for a joint pilgrimage to Trier with
Trithemius during the upcoming Frankfurt trade fair:

"Amplius calamum in praesentia non extendam; mercator enim sum, non notarius"
(I won't extend my pen further at present; for I am a merchant, not a notary)

Vigilius has purchased cosmographic and historiographic works. A footnote
indicates Celtis declined this invitation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
LETTER 92: Johannes Trithemius to Konrad Celtis
Date: May 4, 1495
Location: Sponheim (via Bingen)
Source: Cod. ep. V, 6, fol. 44TV
Pages: 152-153
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 152] Full salutation:
"Frater Johannes Tritemius, abbas Spanhamensis, doctissimo viro Conrado
Celti Protucio, ordinario Ingolstatino, poetae laureato, amico optimo,
ex Spanham salutem et charitatem."

Opening:
"In primis Deum oro, ut valeas, optime Celtis; deinde scias velim, quod
nomen tuum non cesso divulgare doctis et studiosis"
(First I pray God that you are well, excellent Celtis; then I would have
you know that I do not cease to publicize your name among the learned and
studious)

The letter recommends J. Well [Johannes de Lorch, rector of schools in
Bingen] as a bearer who wishes to pursue humanist studies in Ingolstadt
under Celtis's guidance. Trithemius reiterates an invitation to Celtis.
He relays instructions to bookseller Ryemann regarding Greek works for
the next fair. Mentions planned travel to Cologne.

Closing: "IIII Nonas Maii anno Domini 95" (May 4, 1495)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
LETTER 334: Johannes Trithemius to Konrad Celtis
Date: July 1, 1507
Location: Wuerzburg (Herbipoli)
Source: [from Trithemius's Epistolae familiares]
Pages: 592-595
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the longest and most historically significant of the three direct
Trithemius letters. Written 12 years after the 1495 letters, it describes
the political upheaval that forced Trithemius from Sponheim.

[p. 592] Trithemius explains he had hoped to meet Celtis in person but was
forced to communicate by letter, using Celtis's kinsman Nicolaus Wilandt as
messenger. He reports on antiquities discovered near Olmuetz: ancient coins
found in a weathered vessel by boys playing near a riverbank called Switau
(Marcomannic name), near the fortress Merau belonging to the Bishop of
Olmuetz. Asks Celtis to identify whether a lion or griffin is depicted.

[p. 593] Main subject: The Bavarian War of Succession (1504). Trithemius
recounts how "our most serene king Maximilian of the Romans...declared a
most violent war on the Palatinate" with support from multiple princes.
Forces crossed the Rhine near Mainz and devastated territories around
Aachen, Cologne, and Kreuznach. The abbot feared attacks from hostile
regional powers.

[p. 594] Describes turbulent events during the Palatinate-Hesse conflicts.
Trithemius retreated to "Stauronesum" (Heilig Kreuz/Holy Cross) for
twenty-two weeks during the "Spessart fury." Two monks (Melantius Remitius
Prior and Pingionita Joannes) accused him, favoring a hostile faction.
Details of monastery violations and breaches of ecclesiastical liberty.
Journey to Cologne in July to attend a princely convention with the
Royal Majesty.

[p. 595] Trithemius describes relinquishing the Sponheim abbey and
accepting St. Jacob's abbey in Wuerzburg on October 15th through Bishop
Laurentius (Lorenz von Bibra, Bishop of Wuerzburg):

"Elegi proinde pauper habitare in coenobio magis quam dives conversari
praeter monasticam regulam in saeculo."
(I therefore chose to live poor in a cloister rather than rich among
court flatterers beyond the monastic rule.)

References Psalm 123:7 (the trapped bird / broken snare imagery).

Closing: "Herbipoli" (Wuerzburg), July 1, 1507.

NOTE: The scholarly apparatus references this letter also appearing in
Trithemius's own Epistolae familiares.

Letter 74: Erasmus Australis to Celtis (May 11, 1494, Regensburg)
Page: 123
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Erasmus, a Benedictine monk at St. Emmeram in Regensburg, acknowledges
receipt of gifts including "Collationes" by Trithemius. He thanks Celtis
and requests specific classical texts (Greek vocabulary, Caesar's
commentaries, Herodotus, Boccaccio's De Genealogia deorum).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 78: Rutgerus Sicamber to Celtis (June 30, 1494, Heyna)
Pages: 129-130
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 129] "Frater Ruthgerus Venray, canonicus regularis, [totus tuus]
venerando viro Conrado Celti Protucio, poetae eloquentissimo..."

Sicamber describes a visit to Trithemius at Sponheim:
"Veni sane, vidi et expertus sum mirabilia, simul cognovi rumorem re ipsa
multum fuisse inferiorem"
(I came, saw, and experienced marvels, learning the rumor fell far short
of reality)

[p. 130] Trithemius "commended" Celtis warmly, praising him as "a most
eloquent orator, an incomparable poet" and "magnificent master of all arts."
Mentions Trithemius published "De laudibus sanctissimae matris Annae"
(Leipzig 1494). Sicamber seeks Celtis's friendship based on Trithemius's
recommendation.

Closing: "Vale. Ex Heyna octavo Nonas Julias anno 94" (June 30, 1494)

Biographical note: Rutgerus Sicamber (d. 1525), regular Augustinian canon
at Heyna near Worms, noted humanist, matriculated at Cologne (May 4, 1491),
later university rector (1519).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 99: Henricus de Binaw to Celtis (November 30, 1495, Worms)
Pages: 163-164
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 163] "Musarum sponso domino Conrado Celti laureato philosopho et amico
suo charissimo." Binaw requests Celtis's Greek grammar be copied and sent.
Annotation references Trithemius's Melpomenecon dedicated to Binaw
(Mainz 1496).

[p. 164] Heinrich von Buenau thanks Celtis for invitation to join the
Heidelberg "Academia Platonica."

Letter 102: Jacobus Wimphlingus to Celtis (January 4, 1496, Schlettstadt)
Page: 169
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Discusses mutual acquaintances. Footnote references Trithemius's Catalogus
illustrium virorum and De scriptoribus ecclesiasticis as sources on
Leontorius (Cistercian monk, ca. 1460-1511), who was friends with
"Reuchlin, Dalberg, Wimpheling, Trithemius u.a."

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 107: Johannes Vigilius to Celtis (April 19, 1496)
Pages: 178-179
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 178] Vigilius describes a month-long journey to the Frankfurt book fair,
traveling with Jacob Wimpfeling. Reports purchasing Greek, Latin, and Hebrew
texts for several patrons including "Johanne Tritemio" (John Trithemius)
and "Henrico de Bonaw" (Henry of Buenau).

Describes visiting Gresemund in Mainz, Trithemius at Sponheim, and Jakob
Koebel in Oppenheim. A significant issue involves "Cicero-Handschrift
Dalbergs" (Dalberg's Cicero manuscript) that Koebel allegedly lent without
authorization.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 118: Jacobus Dracontius to correspondent (June 29, 1496)
Page: 196
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Footnote provides crucial context from Trithemius's "Chronicon Sponheimense":
On July 4, 1496, several distinguished visitors came to the Sponheim
monastery to view the library, including Johannes Reuchlin, the Bishop of
Worms, and others:

"Hic omnes viri doctissimi visa tanta multitudine rarorum voluminum...
nimium obstupuerunt"
(These learned men were astounded by the great quantity of rare volumes)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 133: Arnold Bostius to Celtis (October 23, 1496, Bruges)
Page: 217
----------------------------------------------------------------------

From a Carmelite monk in Bruges. Discusses the significance of the Epithoma
in utramque Ciceronis Rhetoricam for German humanism. References the death
of Rudolf Agricola and mentions Johannes Trithemius. Bostius was connected
with Trithemius, Erasmus, and other humanist scholars through correspondence.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 147: Arnold Bostius to Celtis (February 8, 1497, Ghent)
Pages: 245-246
----------------------------------------------------------------------

[p. 245] Bostius had read the "Vita Sebaldi" and mentions of the poet in
Hartmann Schedel's chronicle. He expresses regret that works listed by
Trithemius remain unprinted.

[p. 246] Explicit Trithemius reference:
"O cur igitur non adeunt impressa singularia opera tua, a domino Johanne
Tritemio in utroque suo cathalogo iam pridem patefacta, saltem titulo tenus?"
(Oh why then do your singular works not appear in print, already made known
by Lord Johannes Trithemius in both his catalogues, at least by title?)

Footnotes identify the two catalogues as:
- Liber de scriptoribus ecclesiasticis (Mainz 1494)
- De luminaribus sive de viris illustribus Germaniae (Mainz 1495)

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Letter 223: Rutgerus Sicamber to Celtis (September 1, 1499, Sponheim)
Pages: 371-372
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Written FROM Sponheim (Trithemius's monastery). Sicamber writes:

"Tritemius noster, omnium studiosorum amicus, qualis et quis sis, iam
pridem mihi innotuit"
(Our Trithemius, friend of all scholars, has long since made known to me
what and who you are)

Sicamber thanks Celtis for friendship, discusses the presence of Johannes
Reuchlin among them, and asks Celtis to write letters to strengthen their
literary relationship. He modestly deflects praise of his own verses,
calling them "illepidos et pueriles" (inelegant and childish).

NOTE: This letter was written at Sponheim, the very monastery where
Trithemius was abbot and where he wrote the Steganographia (c. 1499).

================================================================================
NOTES ON THE CELTIS-TRITHEMIUS RELATIONSHIP
================================================================================

Based on the extracted material, the relationship between Konrad Celtis and
Johannes Trithemius can be characterized as follows:

1. CLOSE PERSONAL FRIENDSHIP: Trithemius writes "tuus sum et ego totus et
   mea omnia" (I am entirely yours, and all I have is yours) in Letter 88.
   He constantly publicizes Celtis's name "doctis et studiosis" (among the
   learned and studious) per Letter 92.

2. INTELLECTUAL EXCHANGE: Celtis taught Trithemius Greek. Trithemius was
   studying Greek under Reuchlin (mentioned in Letter 88). They exchanged
   books and manuscripts constantly.

3. LITERARY SOCIETY: Both were members of the Sodalitas Litteraria Rhenana
   (Rhenish Literary Society), centered around Johann von Dalberg, Bishop
   of Worms. Other members included Johannes Reuchlin, Jakob Wimpfeling,
   Johannes Vigilius, Rutgerus Sicamber, and Dietrich Gresemund.

4. MUTUAL PROMOTION: Trithemius listed Celtis's works in both his
   Catalogus illustrium virorum (Mainz 1495) and De scriptoribus
   ecclesiasticis (Mainz 1494). Celtis reciprocated with poetic
   dedications.

5. TRITHEMIUS AS HUB: The Sponheim monastery under Trithemius was a major
   intellectual center. Multiple correspondents describe visiting the
   library with astonishment. Reuchlin, Dalberg, and other scholars
   visited regularly.

6. CHRONOLOGICAL GAP: After the 1495 letters (88, 92), there is a 12-year
   gap before the 1507 letter (334). This corresponds to the period during
   which Trithemius wrote the Steganographia (c. 1499) and faced growing
   controversy. Whether intervening letters existed but are lost, or
   whether the friendship cooled during this period, is unclear from the
   available evidence.

7. THE 1507 LETTER AS APOLOGIA: Letter 334 reads as a lengthy self-
   justification for Trithemius's departure from Sponheim, describing
   political upheaval, monastic treachery, and his choice of poverty at
   St. Jakob in Wuerzburg. It may represent an attempt to reconnect with
   Celtis after the gap.

================================================================================
STEGANOGRAPHIA NOTE
================================================================================

No explicit mention of Trithemius's Steganographia (written c. 1499,
circulated in manuscript, not printed until 1606) was found in the
extracted portions of this correspondence. This is notable but not
surprising:

- The Steganographia was controversial and circulated privately
- Trithemius was accused of black magic after its contents became known
- The work may have been discussed in person rather than in writing
- Letter 223 (Rutgerus Sicamber, Sep 1499, FROM Sponheim) was written
  at the very time and place the Steganographia was being completed
- The 12-year gap in direct Celtis-Trithemius correspondence (1495-1507)
  covers exactly the Steganographia period

The full volume contains many more pages referencing Trithemius (see
Register entry above) that have not been extracted here. A complete
review of all 40+ Trithemius reference pages might reveal indirect
allusions.

SODALITAS LITTERARIA RHENANA - KEY MEMBERS IN THIS CORRESPONDENCE
================================================================================

The following members of the Rhenish Literary Society appear frequently
in Celtis's correspondence alongside Trithemius:

- Johann von Dalberg (Bishop of Worms) - patron, Cicero MS owner
- Johannes Reuchlin - Greek scholar, visited Sponheim
- Jakob Wimpfeling - Schlettstadt humanist, preacher in Speyer
- Johannes Vigilius - Frankfurt merchant, book buyer for the circle
- Rutgerus Sicamber - Augustinian canon at Heyna near Worms
- Dietrich Gresemund Jr. (1475-1512) - dedicated works to Trithemius
- Arnold Bostius - Carmelite in Bruges, extensive correspondent
- Erasmus Australis - Benedictine at St. Emmeram, Regensburg
- Heinrich von Buenau - Saxon diplomat, invited to Academia Platonica
- Jakob Koebel - Oppenheim printer/scholar
