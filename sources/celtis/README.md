## Trithemius Correspondence

Johannes Trithemius (1462-1516), Benedictine abbot of Sponheim and author of the Steganographia (c. 1499), was a close friend and correspondent of Celtis. Both were members of the Sodalitas Litteraria Rhenana (Rhenish Literary Society) centered around Johann von Dalberg, Bishop of Worms.

### Direct Trithemius Letters (3 preserved)

| Letter # | Date | From | Location | Pages |
|----------|------|------|----------|-------|
| 88 | 1495-04-11 | Trithemius to Celtis | Frankfurt a.M. | 144-147 |
| 92 | 1495-05-04 | Trithemius to Celtis | Sponheim/Bingen | 152-153 |
| 334 | 1507-07-01 | Trithemius to Celtis | Wuerzburg | 592-595 |

### Key Themes in the Trithemius Letters

1. **Letter 88 (1495):** Requests Greek books, reports studying Greek under Reuchlin, discusses literary society affairs, defends Bishop of Worms. Contains the declaration "tuus sum et ego totus et mea omnia" (I am entirely yours).
2. **Letter 92 (1495):** Recommends a student for Celtis's instruction at Ingolstadt, publicizes Celtis's reputation, arranges book purchases.
3. **Letter 334 (1507):** Major autobiographical letter describing Trithemius's forced departure from Sponheim during the Bavarian War of Succession (1504), monastic treachery, and voluntary exile to St. Jakob in Wuerzburg. The longest of the three.

### Trithemius References Throughout the Volume

The Register (p. 677) lists Trithemius on pages: XI, 48, 60, 63, 69, 90ff., 106, 121, 123f., 129f., 144f., 152f., 163f., 169, 172, 178ff., 183f., 196ff., 217ff., 235, 245ff., 257, 293, 371f., 448, 462, 468, 516, 540, 544, 549, 592ff., 611

Many references are in editorial footnotes citing Trithemius's published works:
- De scriptoribus ecclesiasticis (Mainz 1494)
- Catalogus illustrium virorum / De luminaribus sive de viris illustribus Germaniae (Mainz 1495)
- Chronicon Sponheimense
- Epistolae familiares

### Notable Absence: Steganographia

No explicit mention of Trithemius's Steganographia was found in the extracted portions. The work was written c. 1499 and circulated privately in manuscript (not printed until 1606). The 12-year gap in direct Celtis-Trithemius correspondence (1495-1507) covers the exact period of its composition and the resulting controversy.
