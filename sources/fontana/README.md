# Giovanni Fontana's Cipher Manuscripts

Giovanni (Johannes) de Fontana (c. 1395 -- c. 1455), Venetian physician and engineer, University of Padua (arts degree 1418, medicine 1421). Two of his six known works use an invented cipher script. Both are relevant to Voynich manuscript studies as contemporaneous examples of 15th-century cipher manuscripts from the Veneto region.

---

## Manuscript 1: Bellicorum instrumentorum liber (c. 1420)

### Shelfmark and Location

- **Shelfmark:** Cod.icon. 242
- **Repository:** Bayerische Staatsbibliothek (BSB), Munich
- **Material:** 72 parchment folios, 26.5 x 19.5 cm
- **Language:** Latin (partially in cipher)
- **Date:** c. 1420--1430

### Digital Access

- **BSB Digitale Sammlungen:** https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/details/bsb00013084
  - 149 digital scans, freely accessible
  - License: Public Domain Mark 1.0 Universal
  - Digitized: 31 July 2007
  - IIIF viewer and PDF download available
- **Wikimedia Commons:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Bellicorum_instrumentorum_liber_cum_figuris

### Content

Approximately 140 illustrations of siege engines, fountains, pumps, lifting machines, defensive towers, dredges, combination locks, battering rams, rocket-propelled craft, scaling ladders, measuring instruments, alchemical furnaces, clockwork, and robotic automata. Includes the first known depiction of a magic lantern.

Each illustration is accompanied by a caption typically beginning with a sentence in plaintext Latin, with the remainder in cipher. Example from f. 70r (per Battisti & Saccaro Battisti decipherment): "Apparentia nocturna ad terrorem videntium" ("nocturnal appearance to terrify viewers"), describing a magic lantern projector.

### Cipher Distribution

Mixed plaintext and ciphertext. Introductory sentences are typically in standard Latin; the technical descriptions that follow are enciphered. A cipher alphabet key was added to a foreleaf (not by Fontana himself but by a later hand).

## Manuscript 2: Secretum de thesauro experimentorum ymaginationis hominum (c. 1430)

### Shelfmark and Location

- **Shelfmark:** NAL 635 (Nouvelles Acquisitions Latin 635)
- **Repository:** Bibliotheque nationale de France (BnF), Paris, Departement des manuscrits
- **Material:** 140 parchment folios
- **Language:** Latin (almost entirely in cipher)
- **Date:** c. 1430
- **Attribution:** Joannes Fontana (uncertain; marked with "?" in some catalog entries)

### Digital Access

- **Gallica (primary digitization):** https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b10023795x
  - Freely accessible
  - Public domain
- **Gallica (alternate digitization, from surrogate):** https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b100331057
- **Europeana record:** https://www.europeana.eu/en/item/9200519/ark__12148_btv1b100331057

### Content

A treatise on mnemonic devices and memory techniques. Describes "the reconstruction of natural memory as a machine, to securely fix, combine and encrypt information." Includes mnemonic machines, optical illusions, and memory instruments. A notable feature is the mnemonic snake device (ff. 29v--31r), with an inscription on its spine reading (per Battisti & Saccaro Battisti): "scias quod ego sum serpens qui circulariter volvor ad memorandum omnia que dia[?] possunt infirmare" ("know that I am a snake turning in circles to remember all those things that could weaken in time").

### Cipher Distribution

The entire manuscript is in cipher except for the table of contents, the title, and the concluding formula. Far more extensively enciphered than the Bellicorum instrumentorum.

## The Cipher System

### Type

Simple monoalphabetic substitution cipher. Not designed for secrecy but as a mnemonic exercise -- the glyphs are constructed to be memorable and learnable, not cryptographically strong. The same cipher is used in both manuscripts.

### Glyph Design

23 symbols represent the letters of the Latin alphabet. All glyphs are constructed from circles and straight lines (tickmarks/strokes). The design follows systematic mnemonic principles:

**Vowels (a, e, i, o, u, y):**
- Each represented by a circle with directional tickmarks
- A and E: tickmarks facing west and east
- E and O: tickmarks facing north and south
- I: simple circle (conceptually "squished")

**Consonants -- mirrored group (b, d, p, q):**
- These morphologically similar Latin letters use horizontal mirroring with extended strokes and directional tickmarks corresponding to their ascenders/descenders

**Consonants -- stroke-count group (h, k, m, n):**
- Circles with attached strokes, where the number of strokes corresponds to the number of strokes in the standard alphabet letter

**Other consonants:**
- S: two closed loops (the only double-loop form)
- F: vertical mirroring
- C: mouth shape
- X: cross combined with circle
- Y and Z: angled lines with circles

### Key Features

- The cipher preserves standard medieval abbreviation marks within the enciphered text
- Words are sometimes broken across lines without continuation marks
- Medieval u/v interchangeability applies
- The system was designed for ease of learning and readability, not maximum security
- Pattern recognition alone is sufficient for decipherment even without the alphabet key

## Known Transcriptions and Decipherments

### Battisti & Saccaro Battisti (1984) -- DEFINITIVE FOR BELLICORUM

**Full citation:** Eugenio Battisti and Giuseppa Saccaro Battisti, *Le macchine cifrate di Giovanni Fontana: con la riproduzione del Cod. icon. 242* (Milan: Arcadia Edizioni, 1984). 167 pp., illustrated. ISBN 9788885684065.

**Contents:**
- Facsimile reproduction of Cod.icon. 242 (Bellicorum instrumentorum)
- Complete Latin decipherment of all cipher passages in the Bellicorum
- Parallel translation into modern Italian
- Decipherment of cipher passages from NAL 635 (Secretum) as well
- Extensive scholarly commentary

**Availability:** Out of print. No PDF available online. Available through used book dealers (AbeBooks lists copies) and research libraries. The most comprehensive scholarly treatment of the Bellicorum.

### Kranz (2016) -- DEFINITIVE FOR SECRETUM

**Full citation:** Horst Kranz (ed., trans., intro.), *Methoden des Erinnerns und Vergessens: Johannes Fontanas Secretum de thesauro experimentorum ymaginationis hominum*, Boethius Band 68 (Stuttgart: Franz Steiner Verlag, 2016). 249 pp., 52 b/w illustrations. ISBN 978-3-515-11583-4.

**Contents:**
- Complete Latin transcription of NAL 635 with parallel German translation
- Critical scholarly introduction on the manuscript's history and composition
- Analysis of the cipher alphabet (23 symbols)
- Supplementary illustrations
- Word index, name index, bibliography
- Discussion of Fontana's mnemonic system design

**Availability:** In print from Franz Steiner Verlag, EUR 57.00. E-book PDF available via Biblioscout (DOI: 10.25162/9783515115865). Sample chapter and table of contents available as PDFs from the publisher website: https://www.steiner-verlag.de/Methoden-des-Erinnerns-und-Vergessens/9783515115834

**Note:** The user's query dated this to 2018; the actual publication date is 2016.

### Summary of Decipherment Status

Both cipher manuscripts have been **fully deciphered**. The cipher is a simple monoalphabetic substitution that was solved long ago. Complete Latin transcriptions with scholarly translations exist for both manuscripts:
- Bellicorum: Latin + Italian (Battisti 1984)
- Secretum: Latin + German (Kranz 2016)

No freely accessible online transcriptions of the full deciphered text have been found. The decipherments exist only in the two printed scholarly editions listed above.

## Relevance to Voynich Manuscript Studies

### Similarities (per Pelling, Petersen, Neal, Bax)

- Contemporaneous: Fontana's manuscripts date to c. 1420--1430; the Voynich manuscript's vellum is radiocarbon-dated to 1404--1438
- Same region: Fontana worked in Venice and Padua (northeastern Italy)
- Both use invented glyph systems rather than standard alphabets
- Both contain illustrations alongside enciphered text
- Some illustrative content overlaps: water/bathing scenes, nymphs in fountains, mechanical devices
- Both manuscripts give the impression of "deliberately created puzzles" (Neal)

### Key Differences

- Fontana's cipher is a simple monoalphabetic substitution encoding Latin; the Voynich manuscript's script has not been deciphered and does not yield to simple substitution
- Fontana's cipher was designed to be easy to read; the Voynich manuscript has resisted all decipherment attempts
- Scale: the Voynich manuscript is far larger and more complex
- Fontana's cipher shows systematic regularities (all glyphs built from circles + lines); the Voynich script appears less systematically constructed
- Bax argues the Voynich script is unlikely to be the invention of a single person (as Fontana's was), but rather something that developed more organically

### Scholarly Consensus

Fontana's cipher manuscripts are relevant as **contextual comparanda** -- they demonstrate that the practice of writing entire manuscripts in invented cipher scripts existed in early 15th-century northeastern Italy. However, no scholar has proposed that Fontana authored the Voynich manuscript or that its script derives from Fontana's cipher system. The connection is one of cultural context, not direct linkage.

## Text Extraction Feasibility

**OCR: Not possible.** Both manuscripts use invented cipher glyphs that no OCR system can recognize.

**Manual transcription of cipher glyphs: Already done.** Both Battisti (1984) and Kranz (2016) have produced complete transcriptions by manually reading the cipher glyphs and converting them to Latin alphabet text. These transcriptions exist only in their respective printed/e-book editions.

**Digital text availability: None found.** No freely accessible digital text files of the deciphered Latin content have been located online. Obtaining the deciphered text would require:
1. Purchasing or accessing Kranz (2016) for the Secretum (e-book available, EUR 57)
2. Locating a library copy of Battisti (1984) for the Bellicorum (out of print)
