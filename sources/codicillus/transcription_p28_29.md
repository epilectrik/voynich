## General Observations

1. **Script:** Cursive Gothic bookhand, heavily abbreviated, typical of late 15th century Northern Italian manuscripts
2. **Ink:** Main text in brown/dark ink; chapter headers and key terms in red
3. **Subject matter:** These pages cover sublimation (sublimatio) and circulation (circulatio) — two fundamental alchemical processes
4. **Abbreviation density:** Extremely high. Nearly every word uses standard Latin abbreviations (p with bar = per/pro, q with bar = quod, etc.)
5. **Condition:** Some ink fading, especially at page edges; gutter binding partially obscures inner margin text
6. **Later additions:** 18th-century ownership inscription (Bonaparte, 1713) on preliminary leaf; marginal annotations in different hands on text pages
7. **Confidence:** Due to the dense abbreviation system and cursive hand, approximately 40-50% of individual words are marked uncertain. A specialist in 15th-century Italian Latin paleography would achieve significantly better readings.
