## Translator's Notes

### Color Sequence (the Great Work)
The text describes the classic alchemical color progression:
1. **Nigredo** (blackness) — putrefaction, the "death" of matter (pp. 135-136, 151-153)
2. **Albedo** (whiteness) — washing/purification, achieved through repeated ablution and trituration (pp. 143, 148, 150-155)
3. **Rubedo** (redness) — the final perfection, achieved by continued fire after whiteness is complete (pp. 150-151)

### Key Alchemical Operations Referenced
- **Dissolutio et coagulatio** (dissolve and coagulate) — the fundamental cycle (p. 156)
- **Calcinatio** — reduction to powder by heat (pp. 132, 135)
- **Sublimatio** — purification by vaporization and re-condensation (p. 142)
- **Assatio** — dry roasting (pp. 144, 146)
- **Digestio** — prolonged gentle heating (throughout)
- **Fixatio** — making the volatile permanent (pp. 137, 142)
- **Trituratione** — grinding and pulverizing (pp. 135, 143)
- **Ablutio terrae** — washing of the earth (p. 150)

### Body-Spirit Relationship
The text extensively discusses the interplay between *corpus* (body = fixed principle) and *spiritus* (spirit = volatile principle). The central doctrine: dissolution of the body IS the congelation of the spirit, and vice versa (p. 142). Neither must exceed the other in quantity.

### The Stone and Elixir
The *lapis* (stone/philosopher's stone) is described as passing through sulfurous forms before becoming *elixir* (pp. 138, 161-162). It is extracted from the "two luminaries" (gold and silver, p. 162) and must achieve both white and red forms.

### Manuscript Condition
This transcription contains extensive damage and abbreviation. Many passages are only partially recoverable; the [?] markers reflect genuine uncertainty in the source reading. The translation attempts to follow the sense of the argument even where individual words are uncertain.
