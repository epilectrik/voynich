## Summary of Content (Pages 41-55)

### Chapter Structure Identified:
1. **{red: De Lapide Philosophorum}** (p.41) — "On the Philosopher's Stone" — Opens the practical methodology section with invocation of the Trinity
2. **{red: De Reuelatione nostre Intentionis}** (p.43) — "On the Revelation of Our Intention" — States purpose of the compendium, addressed to "henricum" (Henry)
3. **{red: De forma nostre presentis scientie}** (p.43) — "On the Form of Our Present Knowledge" — Tripartite structure of the work
4. **{red: De Repositione nostri et de contentis materie}** (p.43) — "On the Arrangement and Contents of the Matter"
5. **{red: De primis naturalibus primi gradus et de qualitatibus}** (p.44) — "On the First Natural [principles] of the First Degree and on Qualities" — Sulfur and argentum vivum (mercury) as the two natural principles of metals
6. **{red: De operibus nostris demonstrationis}** (p.46) — "On the Works of Our Demonstration" — Color sequence as signs of progress
7. **{red: De principio tertii}** (p.47) — "On the Beginning of the Third [part]" — Three core operations
8. **{red: De corruptione materie}** (p.48) — "On the Corruption of Matter" — Theory of corruption, heat, and color change

### Key Technical Content:

**Dedication and Address:**
- Dedicated to the King of England ("Regi Anglie"), p.41
- References King Edward ("Regi Odoardo") who swore an oath ("stricto iuramine") to protect the work, p.42
- Later addresses "fili Carissime" (dearest son), p.52 — standard Pseudo-Lull dedicatory formula

**Two Natural Principles (p.44):**
- Sulfur and argentum vivum (quicksilver/mercury) are the *naturalia* of metals
- All metals formed from these two principles in varying degrees of purity

**Color Sequence (p.46):**
- Four colors as signs of transmutation progress:
  - **Nigredo** (blackness) -> **Albedo** (whiteness) -> **Citrinus** (yellowness) -> **Rubedo** (redness)
- Nigredo transmutes into albedo, then into citrinum, then into rubedo
- Rubedo is the "vltimum" (final) demonstration

**Three Core Operations (p.47):**
- **Soluere** (dissolve), **Lauare** (wash), **Rediuum/Fixare** (reduce/fix)
- Marginal note in different hand: "opera ch- / d o / d o s" — possibly cross-referencing cipher letters

**Corruption Theory (pp.48-51):**
- Extended discussion of corruption as a necessary stage
- Color (calor/color) as indicator of the process state
- Analogy to human digestion: as a sleeping man's body digests food through internal heat, so the material undergoes internal transformation
- Death (mors) of the material is not true destruction but a necessary precursor to renewal (reformatio)
- The corruption must be gradual, not violent ("lenta sed in renouata")

**Metals Hierarchy (p.54):**
- **Purest:** Sol (gold) and Luna (silver)
- **Intermediate:** Other metals with varying sulfur/mercury purity
- **Most corrupt:** Marchesita (marcasite), magnesia, tutia (zinc oxide)
- **Even more corrupt:** Salis (salts), atramenta (vitriols), alumina (alums)
- Media between metals and stones

**Cipher Letter References (p.55):**
- Letters **B**, **g**, **m**, **r**, **y** used as operational grade references
- These reference the cipher alphabet system established on pp.36-40
- B = the complete work; g = prima malia (gold, silver); m = principia (sulfur, mercury); r = mercury in fluid state
- Hierarchical: g -> m -> r represents descent from finished metals to raw principles
