## Content Summary

A systematic survey of seven *artes magicae* (forbidden divinatory arts), organized around the classical scheme:

1. **Nigromancy** (necromancy / black magic) -- conflated with witchcraft
2. **Geomancy** -- divination by earth
3. **Hydromancy** -- divination by water
4. **Aeromancy** -- divination by air
5. **Pyromancy** -- divination by fire
6. **Chiromancy** -- palm reading
7. **Spatulamancy** -- divination from shoulder blades

The second through fifth arts correspond to the four elements. Hartlieb promised to cover 84 additional arts beyond these seven, but no surviving manuscript contains this additional material.

The text contains the **oldest known description of witches' flying ointment**. Hartlieb wrote ostensibly as a moralist condemning the arts as demonic, while simultaneously providing vivid, detailed descriptions with numerous practical examples -- creating an instructive tension between didactic warning and implicit handbook.

## About Johannes Hartlieb

Johannes Hartlieb (c. 1410 -- 18 May 1468) was a physician and author in late medieval Bavaria, probably from a family in Neuburg an der Donau. He obtained a bachelor's degree in 1432, a master's by 1437, and a medical doctorate at Padua in 1439. He served the courts of Louis VII of Bavaria, Albert VI of Austria (1430s), Albert III of Bavaria-Munich (from 1440), and Duke Sigismund (from 1456). Beyond the *Buch aller verbotenen Kunst*, he authored a compendium on herbs (c. 1440) and German translations of classical and medieval authors (Trotula, Macrobius, Gilbertinus, Muscio).
