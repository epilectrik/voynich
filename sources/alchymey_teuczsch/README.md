## Content Summary

A compilation titled *Cisioianus; Sammlung alchemischer und medizinischer Rezepte und Traktate* (Collection of alchemical and medical recipes and treatises). Contents include:

- **Alchemical recipes** -- transmutation procedures, especially gold multiplication (dated 1423 within text)
- **Medical remedies** -- treatments for bloating, poisoning, and other ailments
- **Magical texts** -- fever charms (attributed to Michel Wulfing), love magic, blessing formulas
- **Astrological calendar** -- indicating optimal days/times for alchemical work (*Cisioianus*, *Verworfene Tage* / unlucky days)
- **Correspondence templates**
- **Astronomical text**

**Only the alchemical portions are enciphered.** The medical, magical, and calendrical texts are in plaintext.

## The Cipher System

The Bavarian alchemists used **three different cipher alphabets** to encode parts of their work:

- Sometimes individual words are enciphered, sometimes whole recipes
- The cipher involved rearranging the standard alphabet so readers would not recognize it as such
- In addition to letter substitution, the authors employed **coded language** (*verdeckte Rede*): substituting standard chemical terms with secret ones -- e.g., gold = "yellow earth" (*gelbe Erde*), silver = "white earth" (*weisse Erde*), saltpeter = "salt stone" (*Salzstein*)
- The authors provided **direct hints within the manuscript** about how the cipher could be deciphered (fol. 5v and elsewhere)

The cipher was deciphered by **Gerhard Eis** (see publications below).

## About Gerhard Eis

Gerhard Eis (1908-1982) was a German medievalist and historian of medicine, professor of Germanic studies at Heidelberg University. He is considered the founder of German *Fachprosaforschung* (specialized/technical prose research). His key works on alchemical literature include *Vor und nach Paracelsus: Untersuchungen uber Hohenheims Traditionsverbundenheit und Nachrichten uber seine Anhanger* (Stuttgart: G. Fischer, 1965) and the collected *Medizinische Fachprosa des spaten Mittelalters und der fruhen Neuzeit* (Amsterdam: Rodopi, 1982).
